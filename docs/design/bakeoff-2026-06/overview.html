5 directions · 5 independent UX judges · 8 criteria · grounded in the real build (Day 42 · Mile 1438 · NY/CT corridor)
🥇

Winner — D3 · Today (Calm) 325 / 400

A paper-quiet, glanceable dashboard. It won 4 of the 5 judges; the lone dissenter still ranked it second. For a tired, cold-handed hiker in sun glare on one bar of signal, it surfaces the thing that keeps you safe in a two-second glance — safety wins by size, not by a red alarm — while telling the plain truth about uncertain data.

“The one I'd actually carry: I can read the numbers that keep me safe — water, weather, readiness — in a two-second glance, and it tells me the water's unconfirmed in plain words.” — Hal (“Dad”), the end-user judge

03 Recommendation — ship D3, then assemble

D3 is the spine. Its three weakest criteria are the three the other directions each win — so this isn't a compromise, it's an assembly. Take these into the calm dashboard:

From D4 · One Thumb

The thumb-zone targets

Replace D3's tiny text switcher with a persistent, large, bottom-anchored control — consider the command-arc fan for primary actions (Ask · Check-in · Water · Verse). Patches D3's lowest non-delight score. Take the buttons; leave the swipe-only IA.

From D5 · Field Notes

Handwritten honesty + warmth

Keep D3's calm safety hierarchy, but borrow the human voice for the candidate-grade caveat and add a journal/keepsake surface under Trail (the family follows; Father's Day is Jun 21). Patches Delight 6.0 without hurting legibility.

From D1 · Scout Hub

Receipt machinery + real chat one layer down

D3's “glance, don't explore” makes Scout thin. Keep the calm Ask-Scout card as the entry, wire in D1's built-out source-receipts + confidence badge, and let it open into a genuine chat for follow-ups. Glance by default, converse on demand.

From D2 · Trailhead

The idea, not the screen

Location-as-context — Scout answers anchored to a place (“about Morgan Stewart Shelter, mi 1442.6”) and a clean topo “where am I” map — is worth keeping as the Map pillar's deeper layer. Just don't make a dark map the home of the whole app.

04 The panel — every judge, every verdict

Five independent UX-expert personas, each scoring all five directions blind to the others, then naming a winner.

Tess — Touch & Ergonomicspicks D1

Ex-Apple HIG: weights one-handed reach, thumb zones, Fitts's-law target sizes, and gesture cost above beauty.

D1 · 62The textbook-correct thumb layout — full-width bottom tabs + a bottom composer put every primary action in the thumb arc. Flaw: secondary pills under the 44px floor.
D2 · 47Gorgeous cartography sabotaged by the thumb: a 5px drag grabber and Scout-from-a-tiny-pin hide critical actions behind cold-hands-hostile gestures.
D3 · 58Calm and beautifully legible to read, but navigation is tiny centered text-links and 5px dots — under-engineered for a cold thumb.
D4 · 57The biggest, best-placed action targets in the field (the arc), but gambles the whole IA on an undiscoverable swipe with no fallback.
D5 · 49Warm, with a solid full-width tab bar — but Caveat handwriting and tiny stencil labels fail the sun-glare, tired-eyes test.

Overall: d1 ships — every primary action in a persistent, full-width, large-target bottom zone with no gesture to discover. “d4 has the best action surface; I'd graft it onto d1's persistent nav, but alone its gesture-only IA is too risky for a 62-year-old offline on a ridge.”

Rune — Calm Minimalistpicks D3

Rams / Reichenstein restraint: weights signal-to-noise, cognitive load, one focus per screen — but minimalism that hides safety info is punished, not rewarded.

D1 · 51Competent, honest, conventional — but a chat log is the wrong home for a glance; the answer that matters is buried below a question.
D2 · 41Beautiful cartography, but a dark map + pulsing dot + draggable sheet is the opposite of calm under sun and fatigue.
D3 · 67The one screen that respects a tired mind: one focus, huge type, safety winning by size — honest without shouting.
D4 · 49Brilliant thumb ergonomics around a navigation model a tired hiker has to be taught — gesture-only nav is a memory tax, not a calm.
D5 · 48The most soulful and most decorated — washi tape and rotated slips are charm that taxes the eye the brief asked me to protect.

Overall: d3 is the clear ship — the only direction where the safety verdict wins by size, not red or ornament, reads in two seconds, and keeps offline honesty on every screen. “Fix the switcher to large tappable zones and it's both the calmest and the safest.”

Mara — Field & Accessibilitypicks D3

Outdoor-conditions + a11y: grades sun-glare contrast, cold/gloved-hand targets, motor tolerance, offline honesty, and whether the candidate-water caveat is unmissable.

D1 · 50Honest, familiar, high-contrast — but safety lives in chat you have to scroll and ask for, in low-contrast amber-on-cream below the fold.
D2 · 38Beautiful dark cartography that fights every real condition: drag gestures, 10px labels in glare, safety hidden in a peeked sheet.
D3 · 67The two-second cold-glance dashboard: huge type, dark-on-cream contrast, scroll-only, candidate-grade flagged structurally on every screen.
D4 · 51Genuinely engineered for cold hands and sun (arc, dusk mode, big numerals) — but bets the IA on swipes that fail exactly when hands are cold.
D5 · 45The most loved object and the least safe in glare: it encodes the water caveat as a charming handwritten “?” on noisy paper.

Overall: d3 — the only direction where safety-critical info wins by sheer size and structure, contrast is dark-on-cream throughout, and interaction is pure scroll (most motor-tolerant). “d5, while most beloved, literally buries the water caveat in handwriting on low-contrast paper.”

Ivo — IA & Productpicks D3

Norman / Cooper: weights whether the structure maps to the five real jobs with minimal navigation and a sound mental model; punishes cleverness that costs findability.

D1 · 49A sound, instantly learnable 4-tab structure undermined by making a chat log the home a tired hiker can't glance.
D2 · 43Map-as-home is defensible for water/shelter, but a pin-summoned Scout and sheet-as-everything leave a real findability hole.
D3 · 66The cleanest structure-to-job map in the set: each pillar one calm scroll, safety wins by size, the tab model keeps findability intact.
D4 · 55Beautiful glance numerals and real dusk thinking, around a swipe-only model that taxes the memory of the user with least to spare.
D5 · 52Emotionally truest to “stay connected / keep faith,” and wisely keeps 4 tabs — but the analog aesthetic softens the safety signal.

Overall: d3 is the only direction where the structure itself does the safety work. “The bolder structures pay for novelty in learnability; d5's gorgeous journal is runner-up but undercuts the candidate-grade honesty that is the actual safety feature.”

Hal — “Dad” (the end user)picks D3

The 62-year-old actually holding the phone — tired, cold-handed, sun in his eyes, one bar — scoring on what helps him find water, weather, and his verse in the next hour without fighting the thing.

D1 · 53The familiar one — I always know where Map, Gear and Trail are, but the Scout chat is busy and the receipts are too small to read in the sun.
D2 · 40A beautiful dark map that looks great on a table and disappears in bright sun, run by gestures I'd never find when I'm tired.
D3 · 67The one I can read at arm's length in the sun — water, shelter and town are big plain numbers and readiness shouts “84 HOLD” without me hunting.
D4 · 52Those big round thumb buttons are the kindest thing here for cold hands — but making me swipe blind between four worlds is asking a tired old man to memorize a maze.
D5 · 57The one that feels made for me and the family — but pretty handwriting and sepia ink are harder to read fast in glare than I'd like.

Overall: d3 is the one I'd carry. “I'd steal d4's big round thumb buttons for its weak bottom switcher and keep d5's handwritten ‘?’ honesty in my heart — but for staying watered and out of a storm today, the calm paper dashboard wins.”
