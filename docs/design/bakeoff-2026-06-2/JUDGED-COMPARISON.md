# Hogg Country — UX Design Bake-off 2026-06-2 · Judged Comparison

**One judge, one user in mind:** Hal — 62, Day 42 of a NOBO thru-hike, mile 1438.0 in the NY/CT corridor. Sun glare, cold hands, one bar of signal, often fully offline. The question every screen has to answer in two seconds: *what do I do next, and is it safe?*

**The headline up front:** None of the five fabricates a readiness or vitals score. Every direction states "No readiness score" in its own language tags and shows only honest, candidate-grade-flagged data with a weather-forward HUD. That is the bar the brief set after the last bake-off shipped an "84 HOLD" number, and all five clear it. So the contest is decided on *usefulness in the moment*, not on a disqualifying lie.

## 02 · Per-direction analysis

### 🥇 M1 · Day Timeline — 88

**What it is:** The day is a vertical timeline of beads — camp pack-up → on-trail → midday water/food window → the afternoon climb → camp → evening journal & verse. A persistent dark-forest **HUD** pins "right now" on every screen: Day 42, mile **1438.0 / 2,197.4**, Done/To go/Today splits, and a progress bar. Below it, weather is a genuinely first-class blue card.

**Why it wins:**
- **Weather + navigation is the strongest in the field (10).** The weather card carries now-temp 71°, High 82°/Low 63°, wind, PM precip 55%, sunset, *and* a plain-language "**What it means:** warm climb early, scattered showers build after 2p — tank up and reach the shelter before the cells," plus a daylight bar showing "7h 16m left." That is the exact synthesis a hiker needs, not just numbers. The Map screen then backs it with a winding trail ribbon and an elevation profile reading +1,700 ft / −640 ft / high pt — real ups and downs.
- **Prepares-for-the-day is best-in-class (10).** The timeline literally is the plan: it sequences the water window, the steepest stretch, the camp arrival before 8:30p sunset, tomorrow's resupply at Pawling. No other direction puts the *order of the day* on screen this clearly.
- **Cited Scout (9)** answers a real decision ("Can I push past Morgan Stewart?") with a "hold near your plan" recommendation, two grounded reasons (candidate-grade water at 1438.4, 55% PM showers over a +1,700 ft climb), numbered receipts (AWOL p.214, FarOut, NWS cached 6:50a) and a "Confidence · Medium" chip. Honest and decision-shaped.
- **Glanceability (9) and outdoor legibility (9):** the HUD's huge Oswald mile numeral and the timeline's "Now" bead are readable at arm's length; dark-ink-on-cream throughout.

**Watch-outs:** The HUD + weather card + timeline stack is the densest design, so first-time scanning is a hair busier than M3. The bottom switcher is small text-and-glyph (a recurring weakness across these mockups). Gear and Bible are solid but conventional rather than standout.

---

### 🥈 M3 · One Calm Scroll — 83

**What it is:** One calm vertical scroll *is* the app — weather → trail-ahead ribbon + elevation → next water/shelter/town → quick gear check-off → verse → journal prompt. Bible, full gear, and the journal keepsake live exactly one tap deeper. Scout is a calm inline ask-bar that expands with cited receipts.

**Strengths:**
- **One-handed/cold-hands is the best in the field (9).** Pure vertical scroll, no horizontal swipe, no drag handles, no tab-hop to complete the day. This is the most motor-tolerant interaction model here, and it directly answers the cold-hands criterion.
- **Glanceability (9) and outdoor legibility (9):** a dark forest-green weather block leads the scroll with 82°, High/Low, wind, rain 55%, sunset, and a plain-language "Storms likely build after 2pm — make miles early… **Treat all water**." High contrast, prominent, safety-forward.
- **In-the-moment (9):** the inline Scout exchange is the most honest water answer of all five — "I'd treat it as uncertain… candidate-grade… a FarOut note two days back called it 'low, barely flowing'… carry enough to reach Morgan Stewart" with four numbered receipts.

**Watch-outs:** Because everything is one scroll, the day's *sequence* isn't modeled the way M1's timeline is (prepares-day 8 vs 10). Documents-the-journey is a prompt at the bottom plus a one-tap-deeper journal — competent but not the spine. Bible and gear are good but live behind a tap.

---

### 🥉 M5 · Trail Logbook — 78

**What it is:** The day as a warm almanac page. Top half = the day ahead (prominent weather, terrain, stops); bottom half = the running log (miles banked, gear checked, a photo, the verse). Turning the page-corner moves between days, so **writing the journey is the spine**.

**Strengths:**
- **Documents-the-journey is the best here (10).** The journal is not an afterthought — it's the architecture. Past pages show logged miles, ascent, camp, the entry, family hearts ("Mom replied: 'Praying for you'"), Father's Day Jun 21. This is the emotional core of the product, realized.
- **It explicitly solves the warmth-vs-legibility trap** that sank "Field Notes" in the last bake-off: its own language says warmth comes "from layout and one clay accent — never from low contrast, sepia, or handwriting in body text." So it keeps the journal soul *and* a high-contrast ledger. Weather card, ribbon + elevation (with ascent/descent/high-pt), and Bible (verse-of-day + search results) are all strong.

**Watch-outs:** The 2-second glance is slightly slower than M1/M3 because the page is a dense ledger with running totals, weather, stops, and the log all stacked (glance 8). Page-turn navigation between days is lovely but adds a model to learn. One-handed (7) is fine but not exceptional.

---

### M2 · Scout-First Cards — 74

**What it is:** Scout is the spine; home is a calm conversation with a **swipeable deck of live glance cards** on top (weather, next water, terrain, gear, verse). Flick through, tap any card to expand into a sourced answer.

**Strengths:** **Cited Scout (9)** is excellent — receipt chips (AWOL, NWS, FarOut) plus a "Well-sourced" grade, and a dedicated expanded weather screen with an hourly precip bar chart (10%→60%→20%) and a Scout note about clearing the ridge before the 3pm cell. Gear screen has a nice inline swap suggestion ("Heavy fleece → sun hoody, −9oz").

**Watch-outs:** The glance deck is a **horizontal swipe** — exactly the cold-hands gesture the brief warns about (one-hand/cold 6). And the home weather is reduced to a small "82°/63°" tile until you expand it, so weather is less immediately prominent than in M1/M3/M5 (wx+nav 7). Glanceability suffers because the key facts are spread across cards you have to flick to (glance 7).

---

### M4 · Living Map — 70

**What it is:** The trail ribbon is home — light, paper-bright, high-contrast — with weather, water, shelter, and town woven onto the line ahead. Tap a pin to ask Scout about that exact spot; gear, Bible, and journal slide up as sheets.

**Strengths:** It correctly **fixes the prior bake-off's fatal dark-map glare problem** — the ribbon is explicitly "light, no dark glare," cream background, forest line with a white casing. Place-anchored Scout is conceptually the best "where am I / what's around me" model, and its cited water answer ("candidate-grade… tank up here… ~2L for the +1,700 ft climb") is genuinely good. The Psalm 23 reading view has the most readable scripture typography of the five.

**Watch-outs:** **Outdoor legibility takes the hit (6):** weather lives in a translucent `rgba(255,253,248,.94)` overlay strip with `backdrop-filter:blur` floating over the map — lower effective contrast than a solid card, and the pin labels are small. **One-handed/cold (6):** the whole model leans on sheet drag-handles (5px grab bars) and slide-up gestures, plus a pulsing-ring animation — the most gesture-dependent direction, which fails exactly when hands are cold. Weather is present but not as commanding as a dedicated card (wx+nav 7).

## 03 · Recommendation — ship M1 as the spine, then graft

**Ship M1 · Day Timeline.** It wins because it does the two things that matter most to a hiker in the moment better than anyone: it makes **weather + navigation a synthesized, plain-language, first-class HUD** (the "what it means" line + daylight-left bar is the single best safety surface in the bake-off), and it is the only direction that puts the **actual sequence of the day** on screen, so Hal always knows what's next. It scores 8 or 9 on everything and 10 on the two highest-stakes criteria, with no fabricated score to penalize.

Then assemble — the losers are a parts bin:

- **From M3 · One Calm Scroll — the pure-scroll motor model and the solid weather block.** M1's one real weakness is interaction density; adopt M3's no-horizontal-swipe, no-drag discipline within each timeline screen, and consider M3's *solid* dark-green weather block treatment as a fallback for the brightest sun (a solid card beats M4's translucent overlay every time). This patches M1's softest area (one-hand 8 → higher) without touching its strengths.
- **From M5 · Trail Logbook — make the journal a real spine, not an evening node.** M1's "documents-the-journey" (8) is its lowest non-gear score. Borrow M5's page-as-keepsake: per-day logged miles/ascent/camp, the photo, the family-follows line and Father's Day callout. Crucially, take M5's *discipline* too — warmth from layout and one clay accent, never sepia or handwriting in body text.
- **From M2 · Scout-First Cards — the hourly precip strip and the receipt/confidence machinery.** Graft M2's hourly rain bar-chart (10%→60%→20% across the afternoon) into M1's weather card so the "showers build after 2p" claim is *shown*, and keep M2's well-built source-receipt + grade chips as the standard for every Scout answer.
- **From M4 · Living Map — the idea, not the home.** Keep place-anchored Scout ("ask about *this* pin") and the light, high-contrast ribbon as M1's **Map pillar's** deeper layer. Do not make the map the app's home, and do not adopt its drag-handle sheets — those are the parts that cost it legibility and cold-hands points.

**In one line:** M1's timeline + HUD is the chassis; bolt on M3's scroll-only calm, M5's journal-as-spine, M2's hourly precip + receipts, and M4's place-anchored Scout as the Map layer.
